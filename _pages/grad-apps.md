These are the notes I put together while figuring out MS/PhD applications. Requirements and dates vary by program.

## Before applying

- Do you want to work in industry or academia? Are you interested in research?
- Think about the time commitment for an MS or a PhD, and how you will fund it.
- Look at the research component of the degree. An industry-oriented master’s and a research degree can serve different goals.

## Shortlisting universities

Start with your area of interest. Look at faculty and labs working in that area, rather than just the university’s overall ranking.

Things I looked at:

- Research fit and the department’s strength in my area
- Public or private university, fees, and funding
- Profiles of previous students
- Alumni outcomes and job prospects

My rough list was around ten universities, split across ambitious, target, and backup options. The number depends on your interests and profile.

Resources from my notes: [r/MSCS](https://www.reddit.com/r/MSCS/), [shortlisting sheet 1](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1W6jVrDrACpQ-QSYp1QmBkATBnXid5GugNBqXU_8bnm0/edit), [shortlisting sheet 2](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1KKkQpndBwOPbz4L2vP05xUBSidassGks_Y14-hDe7Eg/edit).

## Profile and resume

Keep the resume minimal and use your website for the detailed project write-ups.

My notes focused on research experiences, internships, teamwork, papers or preprints, posters, and coursework. For research-focused applications, give enough detail about what you actually worked on and contributed.

## Letters of recommendation

I planned around three letters. People I considered included:

- A research supervisor who knew my work well
- A course instructor, especially if I had also worked with them as a TA
- A supervisor from another research project or internship

Share details of your work: the problem, what you tried, where things failed, how you dealt with it, and how you worked with others.

## Statement of purpose

Build a clear story from your experiences to what you want to work on next. Explain why the particular program fits, including relevant professors or labs.

Don’t just change the university name and submit the same SoP. Get feedback from a few people and leave time to revise. Follow each program’s page limit.

## GRE and TOEFL

Check what each university requires before booking tests. My notes set aside about a month for preparation, with GRE before TOEFL.

For GRE verbal, I used vocabulary practice, flashcards, and reading. For TOEFL, practice reading, listening, writing, and speaking; check any separate speaking requirement for TA positions.

## Timeline

My rough sequence was:

- Decide whether to apply and start shortlisting.
- Prepare for any required tests.
- Read about labs and contact potential research supervisors where relevant.
- Write the first SoP draft and request letters.
- Revise, collect documents, and submit before each program’s deadline.
